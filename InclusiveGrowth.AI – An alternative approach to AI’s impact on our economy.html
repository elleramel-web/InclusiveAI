Huge potential, for bad and good

To state the obvious, AI has huge potential… for bad and good.

What’s true

AI is (rightly) creating fear on safety, massive upheaval on society, and a potential loss of jobs.

Communities (rightly) feel like this major shift is happening to them, without their input, and fast.

These things are true. And we need to invest the time, resources, and expertise for a generational effort to ensure AI is built in humanity’s best interest.

What’s possible, in parallel

Communities have the opportunity to shape the policies, applications, and investment in AI to build a better future.

Build applications that work for us and with us. Build governance that incorporates our perspectives. And build economies, industries and jobs that work for all.

Vision

What if we consistently prepared for technology transition?

What if our regions knew where to invest, what infrastructure to ready?

…and jobs to anticipate?

Flying blind

Communities, investors, and public sector leaders are flying blind on the impact of AI on their economies.

Wild range

Use cases

Government
“Which local industries will AI grow, shrink, or reshape, and when?”

Target incentives, site selection, and business attraction toward sectors with positive AI demand effects. Stress-test budget and tax-base forecasts against AI adoption scenarios.

Investors
“Where is AI creating durable local opportunity worth capital?”

Identify underinvested firms and corridors positioned to benefit from AI adoption. Assess AI risk across loan and investment portfolios concentrated in exposed industries.

Community leaders
“What does AI mean for the jobs and businesses in our neighborhood?”

Bring a neighborhood-level view of AI impact to the table when public and private investments are being decided. Ground community visioning and advocacy in shared, transparent data.

Firm leaders
“How will AI change demand in our industry here, not just nationally?”

Plan hiring, upskilling, and AI adoption with a local lens on competitors and talent supply. Coordinate sector-wide responses through chambers and industry partnerships.

Universities, community colleges, workforce boards
“Which programs should we grow, sunset, or redesign over the next five years?”

Align program portfolios and capital plans with projected regional demand. Build employer partnerships around industries expected to grow with AI.

Philanthropy and regional funders
“Where will our grants have the greatest impact on economic opportunity?”

Direct grantmaking toward the communities and sectors most affected, and use one shared fact base to align multiple funders in a region.
